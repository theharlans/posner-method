# Indiana Harbor Belt R.R. Co. v. American Cyanamid Co.

- **Citation:** Indiana Harbor Belt R.R. Co. v. American Cyanamid Co., 916 F.2d 1174 (7th Cir. 1990)
- **Court and date:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, October 18, 1990
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://www.leagle.com/decision/19902090916f2d117411912
- **Topics:** torts, strict liability, abnormally dangerous activities, negligence

## What the court did

Held that a manufacturer that shipped a hazardous chemical by rail was not strictly liable for a leak at a rail yard; the claim had to proceed, if at all, on negligence.

## The method on display

- Choosing between strict liability and negligence by asking which rule reduces accidents more cheaply. Strict liability makes sense when the harm cannot be prevented by more care but could be reduced by changing where or how much the activity is done.
- Here the leak could have been prevented by ordinary care, and the carrier, not the shipper, controlled the route and handling. Negligence was the better tool.

## Use it for

Strict liability versus fault, allocating liability between parties in a chain, and any "who controls the risk" question.
