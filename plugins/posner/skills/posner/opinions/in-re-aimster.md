# In re Aimster Copyright Litigation

- **Citation:** In re Aimster Copyright Litigation, 334 F.3d 643 (7th Cir. 2003)
- **Court and date:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, June 30, 2003
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://digital-law-online.info/cases/67pq2d1233.htm
- **Topics:** copyright, contributory infringement, preliminary injunctions, technology platforms

## What the court did

Affirmed a preliminary injunction against a file-sharing service. The record was likely to show contributory infringement, the service's encryption did not help it, and it had not shown substantial noninfringing uses.

## The method on display

- Secondary liability as a cost-benefit question. Where a service has both infringing and noninfringing uses, ask what it would cost the provider to eliminate or substantially reduce the infringing uses. A provider that could do so at reasonable cost and does not is exposed to liability.
- Willful blindness counts as knowledge. Designing a system so you cannot see what users do does not avoid liability.

## Use it for

Contributory and inducement liability, platform design, and any "who is the cheaper policer" question in IP.
