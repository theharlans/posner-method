# Northern Indiana Public Service Co. v. Carbon County Coal Co.

- **Citation:** Northern Indiana Public Service Co. v. Carbon County Coal Co., 799 F.2d 265 (7th Cir. 1986)
- **Court and date:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, August 13, 1986
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/799/265/117938
- **Topics:** contracts, force majeure, impracticability, fixed-price contracts, remedies

## What the court did

Held that a utility could not escape a long-term fixed-price coal contract because regulators ordered it to buy cheaper power. The regulatory orders did not prevent it from taking the coal, so neither the force majeure clause nor the doctrines of excuse applied.

## The method on display

- A fixed-price contract is a deliberate allocation of the risk that market prices will move. The party that took that risk keeps it when prices go the wrong way.
- Excuse doctrines are read narrowly so they do not undo the bargain they were meant to protect.

## Use it for

Force majeure, hardship, change-of-law clauses, and long-term license or supply agreements where market rates have shifted.
