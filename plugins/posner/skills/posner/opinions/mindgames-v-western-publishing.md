# MindGames, Inc. v. Western Publishing Co.

- **Citation:** MindGames, Inc. v. Western Publishing Co., 218 F.3d 652 (7th Cir. 2000)
- **Court and date:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, June 22, 2000 (Fairchild, J., dissenting in part)
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://m.openjurist.org/218/f3d/652
- **Topics:** contracts, licensing, lost profits, damages proof

## What the court did

Affirmed summary judgment for the licensee. No renewal fee was owed, and the licensor's claim for lost royalties from a new game was too speculative on the record.

## The method on display

- No flat rule against lost profits for a new business or product, but the claimant must give a reasonable basis for estimating them. A guess is not enough.
- Damages as expected value: when the probability and size of the lost profit cannot be estimated with reasonable confidence, the claim fails.

## Use it for

Lost royalties, new-product damages, and minimum-royalty or best-efforts disputes in license agreements.
