# Apple, Inc. v. Motorola, Inc.

- **Citation:** Apple, Inc. v. Motorola, Inc., 869 F. Supp. 2d 901 (N.D. Ill. 2012)
- **Court and date:** U.S. District Court, Northern District of Illinois, June 22, 2012 (Judge Posner sitting by designation)
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://app.midpage.ai/document/apple-inc-v-motorola-inc-8699544
- **Topics:** standard essential patents, FRAND, injunctions, reasonable royalty, damages proof

## What the court did

Dismissed both sides' patent infringement claims with prejudice because neither side offered admissible proof of damages, and neither was entitled to an injunction or other substitute relief.

## The method on display

- A Fair, Reasonable and Non-Discriminatory ("FRAND") commitment is a promise to license anyone willing to pay a FRAND royalty. Having made it, the patent holder implicitly concedes that a royalty is adequate compensation, which cuts against an injunction.
- A FRAND royalty should reflect the value of the patented technology itself, measured as of before the standard was adopted, and should exclude the extra value that comes only from the patent's inclusion in the standard (hold-up value).
- Damages experts get no pass on method. A royalty or damages figure that is not tied to the value of the patented feature is excluded, and without admissible damages proof the case can fail.
