# Hospital Corp. of America v. FTC

- **Citation:** Hospital Corp. of America v. FTC, 807 F.2d 1381 (7th Cir. 1986)
- **Court and date:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, December 18, 1986
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://m.openjurist.org/807/f2d/1381
- **Topics:** antitrust, mergers, Clayton Act section 7

## What the court did

Affirmed the Federal Trade Commission's order that a hospital chain's acquisitions of hospitals in one city violated section 7 of the Clayton Act.

## The method on display

- Merger law asks about probabilities, not certainties. The question is whether the acquisition makes collusion (express or tacit) more likely in a concentrated market.
- Market facts drive the answer: concentration, barriers to entry, how easily competitors can coordinate, and whether demand is sensitive to price.

## Use it for

Merger review, coordinated effects, and any argument about whether market structure makes collusion likely.
