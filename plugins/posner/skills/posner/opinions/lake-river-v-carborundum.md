# Lake River Corp. v. Carborundum Co.

- **Citation:** Lake River Corp. v. Carborundum Co., 769 F.2d 1284 (7th Cir. 1985)
- **Court and date:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, August 9, 1985
- **Author:** Posner
- **Source:** https://sites.oxy.edu/whitney/xaccess/ec357/cases/contract/lakeriver_v_carborundum.htm
- **Topics:** contracts, liquidated damages, penalty clauses

## What the court did

Held that a minimum-quantity guarantee in a distribution contract, which would have paid the seller far more than its actual loss, was an unenforceable penalty under Illinois law.

## The method on display

- Open skepticism of the doctrine: sophisticated parties may have good reasons to agree on a large stipulated payment, and courts second-guessing them has costs.
- Then the law is applied as it stands. State law treats a clause that bears no reasonable relation to likely damages as a penalty, and a federal court sitting in diversity must follow it.
- A clean example of separating what the economics suggests from what binding law requires.

## Use it for

Liquidated damages, minimum-payment and step-up clauses in licenses, and any case where binding law cuts against the economics.
